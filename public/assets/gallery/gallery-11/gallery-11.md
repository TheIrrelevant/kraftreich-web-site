# Deliverables
deliverables:
  - Created a sequence of painterly travel images structured like stops on a route.
  - "Built each scene around a different travel condition: cave threshold, coastal passage, ruins, river, village, and mountain valley."
  - Used color, light, brush texture, and perspective to make each image feel like a remembered destination.
  - Treated the series as a visual itinerary rather than a set of isolated landscape paintings.
tools: "Procreate"
deliverablesIntro: >
  This art series works like a painted travel route. Each image reads as a stop on a
  journey: a cave entrance, a sea path, an ancient ruin, a river valley, a stone village,
  and a mountain road. The goal was to make the viewer feel movement through place rather
  than simply look at separate scenic images.
deliverablesExtra: >
  The series shifts between natural, historical, and imagined landscapes. Some frames feel
  archaeological, some pastoral, some coastal, and some almost mythic. The shared language
  comes from painterly texture, warm light, deep paths, and compositions that always suggest
  a next destination beyond the frame.

# Team
team: []
teamNarrative: >
  This was created as a personal art series, focused on atmosphere, place-memory, and the
  idea of travel as a sequence of visual thresholds.
teamAttribution: "Personal art series"

# Problem & Solution
summaryStatement: >
  The series turns travel into a sequence of painted thresholds, where every image feels
  like a place arrived at and a path toward somewhere else.
problem: >
  A landscape series can become decorative when every image only describes a beautiful
  location. The challenge was to give the paintings a route logic: a sense that the viewer
  is moving through caves, cliffs, ruins, rivers, towns, and mountains as part of one
  continuous journey.
solution: >
  Each painting was built around a directional cue: a cave path, a bridge, a stone stair,
  a river bend, a village street, or a valley road. These elements pull the eye forward
  and make the image feel navigable. Brush texture and warm natural light keep the works
  tactile, while the changing locations give the series the feeling of an imagined travel
  atlas.
