  My contribution focused on vineyards and wine production as a trade-capable economic

  zones, agro-processing, and wine-related hospitality could reconnect land, production,
  and commerce so reconstruction is not only housing and roads, but a living rural economy.

# Problem & Solution
summaryStatement: >
  Tugh is rebuilt as a regenerative ecovillage where settlement, culture, and vineyard
  production return together, turning Karabakh's wine landscape into both identity and trade.
problem: >
  After decades of conflict and abandonment, Tugh needed more than rebuilt houses. The
  village had to recover civic life, cultural memory, and an economic base strong enough
  to bring people back. Without productive land systems, reconstruction risks becoming
  empty infrastructure. Tugh's historic strength in vineyards and winemaking had to be
  restored as a working landscape, not left as nostalgia.
solution: >
  The SPDO masterplan organizes Tugh as a Cultural and Regenerative Village: adaptive reuse
  and settlement structure restore identity, while agricultural continuity upgrades vineyards
  and agro-processing into contemporary productive systems. My work pushed the wine layer
  further into trade logic — vineyard fields, production clusters, and hospitality nodes
  planned as one economic network — so the rebuilt village can grow, export, host visitors,
  and sustain rural life through Karabakh wine culture again.
