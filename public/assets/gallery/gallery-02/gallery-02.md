# Problem & Solution
summaryStatement: >
  Hadrut is rebuilt as a resilient mountain settlement where memory, agriculture,
  and adaptive urban design restore civic life across a dispersed Karabakh landscape.
problem: >
  After conflict, Hadrut needed more than repaired buildings. The town and its surrounding
  villages had lost continuous settlement life, productive land systems, and public space
  strong enough to hold a returning community. Mountain topography, dispersed villages,
  and layered military, agricultural, and cultural traces made generic reconstruction
  insufficient. Without a clear urban and landscape structure, the region risked becoming
  fragmented infrastructure instead of a living settlement network.
solution: >
  The SPDO masterplan regenerates Hadrut through strategic layering: land-use integration,
  agricultural and industrial revitalization, cultural center recovery, military memory
  as urban identity, and smart settlement systems. Residential zones sit on slope-friendly
  plateaus while flatter land supports agriculture and logistics. My contribution sat in
  the urban design and landscape reading of that plan — streets, terraces, plazas, reused
  stone fabric, and green edges designed so reconstruction returns walkable civic life,
  local economy, and a coherent mountain-town identity.
