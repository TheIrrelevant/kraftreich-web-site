# Deliverables
deliverables:
  - Designed Atrium Serenitatis as a solo architectural concept for a calm urban park.
  - Modeled the courtyard, water voids, steps, walls, and planted islands in AutoCAD and Autodesk 3ds Max.
  - Developed a Tadao Ando-influenced spatial language using concrete planes, water reflection, and controlled emptiness.
  - Used Midjourney and Midjourney Edit as an experimental render and material retexturing pipeline.
  - Created still images and a video presentation from corrected architectural render passes.
tools: "AutoCAD, Autodesk 3ds Max, Midjourney"
deliverablesIntro: >
  Atrium Serenitatis is a solo architectural concept for an urban park that creates a
  quiet inner world inside the city. The project uses concrete, water, bamboo, steps,
  and voids to slow the body down and turn the courtyard into a meditative public space.
deliverablesExtra: >
  The visualization process combined conventional modeling with experimental rendering.
  The space was built in AutoCAD and 3ds Max, then rendered through Midjourney. Texture
  and material corrections were made part by part through Midjourney Edit, allowing stone,
  concrete, water, bamboo, and light behavior to be adjusted separately before the project
  was reassembled as a coherent visual and video sequence.

# Problem & Solution
summaryStatement: >
  Atrium Serenitatis proposes a calm civic void inside the city, using concrete, water,
  bamboo, and stepped geometry to create silence without removing the project from its
  urban context.
problem: >
  Urban parks often depend on softness alone: planting, seating, and open area. This
  project needed a different kind of calm, one created through architectural discipline.
  The challenge was to make a public space feel meditative without becoming empty, cold,
  or disconnected from the city around it.
solution: >
  The design organizes the park as a controlled atrium. Concrete walls define the edge,
  water creates stillness and reflection, bamboo softens the mass, and stepped platforms
  slow movement through the courtyard. The experimental render process supported this
  concept by allowing material tone and texture to be refined in layers, so the final
  images keep the austerity of concrete while preserving atmosphere, light, and public
  usability.
